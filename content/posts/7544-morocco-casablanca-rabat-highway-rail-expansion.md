## 🛣️ Morocco clears the last land for a new Casablanca–Rabat "continental" highway

The Ministry of Equipment and Water has authorized the government to take ownership of the remaining private land needed to finish a new **58.4-kilometer highway linking Casablanca and Rabat** (running from kilometer point 0+000 to 58+367), along with connecting sections of the ring highway around Rabat and Salé. The stretch covered by this specific decision sits in the Chellalate commune of Mohammedia province, where a 245-square-meter farming plot — currently used to grow barley and claimed by several co-owners — is being taken for the project. The wider route also crosses Médiouna and Benslimane provinces (Casablanca-Settat region) and the Skhirat-Témara prefecture (Rabat-Salé-Kénitra region). The road was first declared a project of public utility in 2023, with that declaration renewed in late 2025, meaning construction is now moving from planning into active land acquisition — a concrete sign the project is advancing toward Morocco's broader push to upgrade transport links before the 2030 World Cup.

*Source: Decree 1748.26 of the Minister of Equipment and Water, dated 20 Safar 1448 (4 August 2026).*

## 🚄 Two high-speed rail lines get more land — Tangier-Fes and Kenitra-Marrakech

Morocco is widening its high-speed rail (Al Boraq-style) network on two fronts. Near Tangier, the Ministry of Transport and Logistics authorized taking land parcels in the Al Aouama commune to build a new high-speed rail link for Tangier and to double a 6.3km segment (km 9+922 to 16+231) of the existing Tangier–Fes line. Separately, on the Kenitra–Marrakech high-speed line — the route that will eventually extend Al Boraq service south — the ministry authorized land acquisition in Imzouzene Al Janoubia commune (Settat province) to widen a roughly 800-meter stretch (km 390+847 to 391+647) of the rail corridor's protected right-of-way. Both moves build on public-utility declarations issued in 2023–2024, underscoring that Morocco's rail expansion, including the extension toward Marrakech, is progressing section by section rather than stalling.

*Source: Decrees 1625.26 (23 July 2026) and 1723.26 (29 July 2026) of the Minister of Transport and Logistics.*

## 🌊 Rabat's Bouregreg riverbanks get more room for redevelopment

The Wali (regional governor) of Rabat-Salé-Kénitra has authorized expropriating land parcels in Salé needed to build a development road and a protected natural zone, both part of the long-running master plan for the Bouregreg river valley — the scenic area separating Rabat and Salé that has been the focus of a major urban renewal push for two decades. The decision covers parcels marked "RP 4008" and "TN" on the official zoning plan for the riverbanks, following a public inquiry held in June and July 2026.

*Source: Decree 1655.26 of the Wali of Rabat-Salé-Kénitra, dated 13 Safar 1448 (28 July 2026).*

## 🐟 Two dormant fish farms lose their licenses near Dakhla; a new one opens near Sidi Ifni

Moroccan authorities revoked the operating concessions of two aquaculture farms near Dakhla — "Algues Culture" and "Aquazit" — after inspectors found in April 2026 that both had sat unused for more than a year, triggering a "use it or lose it" clause in Morocco's 1973 fisheries law. In the same batch of decisions, a newly registered company, Sarl Ecosystems Fisheries (based in Agadir), was granted a 10-year, renewable license to build and run a 25-hectare floating-cage fish farm off the coast of Sidi Ifni, raising gilthead sea bream, sea bass and meagre. The company will pay a fixed annual fee of 12,500 dirhams plus 0.1% of the value of fish sold — a small but concrete data point on how Morocco prices its coastal aquaculture concessions.

*Source: Decrees 1537.26, 1538.26 and 1539.26, all dated 13 Safar 1448 (28 July 2026).*
