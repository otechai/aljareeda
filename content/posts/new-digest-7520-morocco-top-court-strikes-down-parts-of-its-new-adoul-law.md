# Morocco's Top Court Strikes Down Parts of Its New Adoul Law

*Sourced from the Kingdom of Morocco's Official Gazette (Bulletin Officiel), issue №7520, dated 25 June 2026 (9 Muharram 1448). This digest translates and prioritizes the parts that actually matter to Moroccans abroad and outside observers — not the routine bureaucratic paperwork.*

## ⚖️ Constitutional Court blocks key parts of the new law on adouls

Morocco's Constitutional Court has ruled that several provisions of Law 16.22, which regulates the adouls, are unconstitutional. Adouls are Morocco's religious-law notaries. The examples in the ruling include marriage contracts and property transfers. The law had passed Parliament, with a final vote on 28 April 2026, but had not yet been put into force when 93 members of the House of Representatives asked the court to review eight of its articles. The court ruled on 15 June 2026. It went further than the challenge and struck down a ninth article on its own initiative, along with two whole chapters in part. It upheld the remaining challenged articles.

**What the court struck down:**

- **Deaf and speech-impaired people (Article 53, first two paragraphs).** The law let adouls take a statement from these citizens directly. A sworn interpreter, a sign-language expert or "any qualified person" would only be used if the adoul decided there was a difficulty. The court said this left a vulnerable group's ability to express its will to the adoul's discretion, with no legal guarantee. That breaks the constitutional promise of real equality and the state's duty to protect people with disabilities.
- **Witness rules for a group-testimony certificate (Article 67, item 1).** The law required at least 12 witnesses, "male and female," for a *lafif* (a certificate backed by a group of witnesses). The court found the phrase too vague. It did not say how many of each sex were needed, and different judges could read it differently. It struck the entire item. Deleting only the phrase would have produced a men-only rule, which was not what lawmakers intended.
- **Conflicts of interest (Article 8).** The article lists jobs an adoul cannot combine with the profession, including commercial activity, managing a company and most other paid private work. It also says any adoul in that situation faces disciplinary penalties. The court found the article gives no deadline to fix such a situation, no procedure to declare it, and no authority to receive the declaration. It called this a legislative omission.
- **The adouls' national body and regional councils (Articles 140–194).** The law set them up but said nothing about what happens if they stop working or cannot meet. The court said this threatens continuity of a public service and leaves users without basic protections.

**A safeguard on discipline (Article 120).** Adouls are disciplined by a committee that the Justice Minister, or a representative, chairs. The court upheld the article on one condition. The minister may carry out the committee's decision but may not revisit, change or replace it.

**What survived:**

- The rule that two adouls must jointly receive a testimony (Article 50).
- The rule allowing a copy of a property document, with a certificate of loss, to be used in a transfer (Article 55).
- The article on registering a property transaction (Article 63), even though critics said it has no way to safeguard the buyer's payment.
- The appeal route against a judge's refusal to certify an adoul's deed (Article 77).

The gazette prints the ruling but does not say what happens next to the law.

<small>Constitutional Court Decision No. 263.26 M.D., 15 June 2026 (29 Dhu al-Hijjah 1447), on Law No. 16.22.</small>

## 🖥️ Debts and "physical coercion": one online platform for payment and tracking

A new government decree sets up an electronic platform inside the Justice Ministry's court portal, mahakim.ma, for handling "physical coercion" (*contrainte par corps*), the court-ordered measure used to enforce unpaid sums. Under the decree, a person subject to a coercion decision can log in to read the enforcement judge's ruling, and creditors who requested coercion can track their requests. Debts can be paid through any payment method the platform offers, and a receipt is issued for each payment. The platform must run every day of the week, and its data must be updated regularly in line with personal-data and IT-security law. Each record must show the debtor's name, date of birth and ID number (electronic national ID, passport, or residence permit for foreigners), the amount owed in words and figures, the type of debt, and the court references. The decree does not set prison terms or amounts. It covers only the platform and the data it holds.

<small>Decree No. 2.26.052, 11 June 2026 (25 Dhu al-Hijjah 1447).</small>

## 🎓 Eighteen foreign degrees added to Morocco's recognition list

For anyone who studied abroad, the Higher Education Minister accepted 18 specific foreign qualifications for equivalence with Moroccan degrees. The list includes 10 master's degrees, 6 bachelor's-level degrees, 1 doctorate and 1 engineering diploma. By country of issue:

- **Canada (4):** Université de Montréal, Université d'Ottawa (two degrees) and McGill.
- **Saudi Arabia (3):** the Islamic University of Medina (two degrees) and the Haram al-Makki Institute.
- **China (2):** Tongji University and Beijing Language and Culture University.
- **Egypt (2):** the American University in Cairo and the Institute of Arab Research and Studies.
- **Mauritania (2):** the University of Nouakchott and Chinguetti Modern University.
- **One each:** Ukraine (Kharkiv Polytechnic, accepted for the state engineer diploma), Switzerland (University of Geneva), the Netherlands (Maastricht University), Türkiye (Istanbul Sabahattin Zaim University, the doctorate) and Malaysia (Universiti Teknologi MARA).

Each decision names one exact diploma and field, so check the specific title before assuming your degree is covered. Individual applications still follow the procedure set by a 2001 decree.

<small>Higher Education Ministry decisions Nos. 1052.26 and 1058.26–1074.26, 2 June 2026 (16 Dhu al-Hijjah 1447).</small>

## 🌿 Advisory council warns Morocco's biodiversity is eroding, with farming and fisheries at risk

The Economic, Social and Environmental Council, a state advisory body, unanimously adopted an opinion on 22 December 2025 calling for a "renewed governance" of biodiversity. It is an advisory opinion, not a law. It counts 25,597 animal species and 7,491 plant and fungus species in Morocco. It also documents losses:

- About 75% of local wheat and barley varieties have disappeared in five decades.
- Average soil organic matter is around 1.3%, which the council says is below the level needed to keep soil fertile.
- In some parts of Souss-Massa, losses are estimated above 80%, linked to plastic-greenhouse farming.
- Up to 22.8% of fish catches are discarded at sea.
- Reforestation has reached 150,000 hectares since 2022, which is 25% of the 600,000-hectare target for 2030.
- Certified organic farmland was about 13,300 hectares in 2025, against a goal of 100,000 hectares by 2030.

The council asks for a biodiversity framework law and for a national committee on climate and biodiversity placed under the Head of Government. It also wants a national biodiversity fund, tougher environmental policing, and restoration of at least 30% of degraded ecosystems by 2030.

<small>Opinion of the Economic, Social and Environmental Council, adopted 22 December 2025 (177th ordinary session), published in issue 7520.</small>

## 🏗️ Land cleared for a Rabat engineering school, a maternity hospital and a road

Three land decisions authorize giving up ownership of plots for public projects.

- **Rabat engineering school.** The Finance Minister authorized giving up co-ownership rights in a 1-hectare, 65-are, 62-centiare plot (about 16,562 m²) for an education facility, the Mohammadia School of Engineers.
- **Rabat maternity hospital.** She did the same for a 9-hectare, 41-are plot (about 94,100 m²).
- **Aït Melloul road.** The mayor of Aït Melloul, in the Inezgane–Aït Melloul prefecture, re-drew the boundaries of road TA558 in the Kasbah Taher district. The order names 2,195 m² of state-owned land and 1,937 m² of an unregistered plot, both bare land, to be expropriated.

In both Rabat cases, the co-owners are listed as having no known address, which will matter to anyone with inherited or shared land.

<small>Finance Ministry decisions Nos. 946.26 and 947.26, 11 May 2026 (23 Dhu al-Qi'dah 1447); Aït Melloul council president's order No. 1045.26, 17 April 2026.</small>
